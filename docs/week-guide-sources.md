# Goldfox week-by-week guide: sources

Every line in `weeks.json` names the sources it comes from (`src`). Each source below was read and checked
in November 2026. Only these sources are used; nothing is taken from general memory.

## How it is written

- It talks to you about your own baby. No "most babies", "many babies", "typical" or other comparisons with other children.
- "It's highly likely you'll see…" is used only for CDC milestones, which at least 3 in 4 children reach by that age.
  Things that vary more say "you may…", or give the full healthy age range (WHO motor windows).
- No checklists or scores: nothing to pass or fail.
- The doctor box appears only for real warning signs (fever under 6 months, urgent crying or vomiting signs, eye alignment
  from 6 months, colic after 4 months) and at the check-up weeks.

Deliberately left out: "Wonder Weeks" / predictable developmental leaps. The idea comes from small studies, and attempts to
replicate it have been mixed and disputed. So leaps are not presented as fact.

Also note: the widely repeated "crying peaks at 6 weeks" is not supported by the largest meta-analysis (Wolke 2017); the guide
says crying stays around 2 hours a day for the first 6 weeks and falls after 8-9 weeks instead.

## Sources

### CDC
- CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." milestone checklists (2021 revision): 2, 4, 6, 9 and 12 months, incl. parent tips
- https://www.cdc.gov/wic-guide/media/pdfs/FULL-LIST-CDC_LTSAE-Checklists2021_Eng_FNL2_508.pdf
- Key facts used: Milestones that MOST children (at least 75%) are expected to meet by each age. Not a screening tool.

### CDC-HELP
- CDC Milestones by age, "How you can help your baby learn and grow" (2 months, 4 months, 6 months, 9 months, 1 year)
- https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-months.html · /4-months.html · /6-months.html · /9-months.html · /1-year.html
- Key facts used: Activity and interaction ideas per age, e.g. respond to sounds to teach turn-taking; cuddle (you cannot spoil a baby by holding); tummy time with toys at eye level; peek-a-boo; copy sounds; drop-and-return game; my turn/your turn; quick cheerful goodbyes; talk about what you are doing; respond with words when baby points.

### WHO-MM
- WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study Group. WHO Motor Development Study: windows of achievement for six gross motor development milestones. Acta Paediatr Suppl 2006;450:86-95
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16817682/ · table: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/indicators/motor-development-milestones/mm_windows_table.pdf
- Key facts used: 1st-99th percentile windows (months): sitting without support 3.8-9.2; standing with assistance 4.8-11.4; hands-and-knees crawling 5.2-13.5; walking with assistance 5.9-13.7; standing alone 6.9-16.9; walking alone 8.2-17.6. 4.3% of children never crawled on hands and knees.

### WOLKE
- Wolke D, Bilgin A, Samara M. Systematic review and meta-analysis: fussing and crying durations and prevalence of colic in infants. J Pediatr 2017;185:55-61. doi:10.1016/j.jpeds.2017.02.020
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28385295/
- Key facts used: Fuss/cry 117-133 min/day (mean) in the first 6 weeks, about 68 min/day by 10-12 weeks; significant drop after 8-9 weeks; no evidence of a universal peak at 6 weeks; colic 17-25% in first 6 weeks, 11% at 8-9 weeks, 0.6% at 10-12 weeks.

### GALLAND
- Galland BC, Taylor BJ, Elder DE, Herbison P. Normal sleep patterns in infants and children: a systematic review of observational studies. Sleep Med Rev 2012;16:213-222. doi:10.1016/j.smrv.2011.06.001
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21784676/
- Key facts used: Infant sleep 12.8 h/day on average (range 9.7-15.9); night wakings 0-3.4 per night at 0-2 months.

### WHO-PA
- WHO guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age (2019)
- https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more
- Key facts used: Infants: active several times a day, floor-based play; at least 30 min tummy time spread through the day while awake; not restrained more than 1 h at a time; screen time not recommended; reading and storytelling encouraged; sleep 14-17 h (0-3 months), 12-16 h (4-11 months) incl. naps; 1-2 years 11-14 h.

### AAP-SLEEP
- Moon RY, Carlin RF, Hand I; AAP Task Force on SIDS. Sleep-related infant deaths: updated 2022 recommendations for reducing infant deaths in the sleep environment. Pediatrics 2022;150(1):e2022057990
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35726558/
- Key facts used: Back to sleep; firm, flat (non-inclined) surface; room sharing without bed sharing; no soft bedding; avoid overheating; human milk feeding, immunisation and a pacifier lower risk.

### AAP-VITD
- Wagner CL, Greer FR; AAP. Prevention of rickets and vitamin D deficiency in infants, children, and adolescents. Pediatrics 2008;122:1142-1152
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18977996/
- Key facts used: All infants, including exclusively breastfed, need at least 400 IU of vitamin D a day beginning soon after birth.

### NIAID-PEANUT
- Togias A, et al. Addendum guidelines for the prevention of peanut allergy in the United States (NIAID expert panel). J Allergy Clin Immunol 2017;139:29-44
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28065278/
- Key facts used: Peanut allergy can be prevented by introducing peanut-containing foods in infancy; separate guidance by risk level.

### AAP-MEDIA
- AAP Council on Communications and Media. Media and young minds. Pediatrics 2016;138(5):e20162591
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27940793/
- Key facts used: Guidance on screen media for children 0-5; emphasises shared use and time for other healthy activities.

### AAP-READ
- High PC, Klass P; AAP. Literacy promotion: an essential component of primary care pediatric practice. Pediatrics 2014;134:404-409
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24962987/
- Key facts used: Reading aloud from infancy supports brain development, language and the parent-child relationship.

### NHS-SOLIDS
- NHS: Your baby's first solid foods
- https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/babys-first-solid-foods/
- Key facts used: Start solids around 6 months; allergens (peanut, egg, gluten, fish) from around 6 months one at a time; no added salt or sugar; avoid whole nuts, raw carrot/apple; quarter grapes and cherry tomatoes; cows' milk not a main drink before 1 year.

### NHS-TEETH
- NHS: Baby teething symptoms
- https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/teething/baby-teething-symptoms/
- Key facts used: Most babies start teething around 6 months; some before 4 months, some after 12 months; temperature may be slightly raised but under 38 C; no evidence teething causes diarrhoea.

### NHS-FEVER
- NHS: High temperature (fever) in children
- https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/
- Key facts used: High temperature is 38 C or more. Get urgent advice if under 3 months with 38 C or higher, or 3-6 months with 39 C or higher. No paracetamol under 2 months; no ibuprofen under 3 months or under 5 kg.

### NHS-SLEEP
- NHS: Helping your baby to sleep
- https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/helping-your-baby-to-sleep/
- Key facts used: Every baby's pattern differs; teach night vs day (low lights, quiet, little play at night); same room as you for at least the first 6 months.

### SG-NCIS
- Singapore National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (NCIS), effective 1 Nov 2020 (SingHealth Polyclinics copy). MOH published an update dated 1 Apr 2026 that could not be read automatically; check it before relying on dose timing.
- https://polyclinic.singhealth.com.sg/content/dam/singhealth-web/shp/patient-services/pdfs/national-childhood-immunisation-schedule-ncis.pdf · update: https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/18/9459eb0d-6797-466d-8d85-274916ebd27e/NCIS_1%20Apr%202026.pdf
- Key facts used: Birth: BCG, HepB 1. 2 m: DTaP-IPV-Hib-HepB (6-in-1) dose 1. 4 m: DTaP-IPV-Hib dose 2 + PCV 1. 6 m: 6-in-1 dose 3 (HepB 3) + PCV 2. Influenza yearly 6 months to <5 years. 12 m: MMR 1, varicella 1, PCV booster. 15 m: MMRV. 18 m: DTaP-IPV-Hib booster.

### AAP-VISION
- AAP HealthyChildren.org: Infant vision development: what can babies see? (adapted from Caring for Your Baby and Young Child, 8th ed., 2024)
- https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/Babys-Vision-Development.aspx
- Key facts used: Newborns see light, shapes, faces and movement; see best at 20-30 cm; eyes may wander or cross, settling by 2-3 months. By 3 months focus on faces and close objects and follow a moving object. By 4 months better colour vision, interest in round patterns, reach for close objects. By 6 months see differences between objects.

### AAP-EYES
- Hill D (AAP). Newborn eye color: what parents should know. HealthyChildren.org, updated 2021
- https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/Newborn-Eye-Color.aspx
- Key facts used: Eye colour takes about a year to settle (change slows after 6 months). Crossing can be normal for the first 6 months. By week 4 the baby focuses on your face when cradled. Until 2 months may lose track of a face crossing the midline; by 2 months tracks side to side. From 6 months, an eye that does not look the same way as the other should be checked by a doctor.

### AAP-HEAR
- AAP HealthyChildren.org: Hearing and making sounds (adapted from Caring for Your Baby and Young Child), updated 2021
- https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/Hearing-and-Making-Sounds.aspx
- Key facts used: Babies prefer the human voice, especially high-pitched, slow, exaggerated speech; by one month identify a parent by voice; about 2 months cooing and vowel sounds; by 4 months babble routinely and are more sensitive to tone of voice.

### AAP-FUSSY
- AAP HealthyChildren.org: Colic relief tips for parents, updated 2024
- https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx
- Key facts used: Regular fussy period, often 6 pm to midnight; carrier, rocking, white noise, pacifier, swaddling may help; it is OK to put the baby down safely and leave the room for a few minutes; never shake a baby.

### AAP-COG
- AAP HealthyChildren.org: Cognitive development: 8 to 12 months (adapted from Caring for Your Baby and Young Child), updated 2023
- https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/Cognitive-Development-8-to-12-Months.aspx
- Key facts used: Attention 2-3 minutes per toy at 8 months, up to 15 minutes by 12 months; drops, rolls and throws things to learn how they behave; object permanence (searches under a scarf at 8 months, keeps looking by 10 months); peekaboo variations; household objects make the best toys; secure hazardous drawers; near 12 months uses props like a toy phone, hairbrush or cup the way adults do.

### NHS-REFLUX
- NHS: Reflux in babies (reviewed June 2025)
- https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/reflux-in-babies/
- Key facts used: Bringing up milk during or shortly after feeds is very common; usually starts before 8 weeks and gets better by age 1 as the food-pipe muscle strengthens; hold upright during and after feeds, burp regularly, keep sleep flat on the back. See a GP if reflux starts after 6 months; urgent advice for green/yellow or bloody vomit, projectile vomiting, blood in poo, swollen tummy, refusing feeds.

### NHS-COLIC
- NHS: Colic (reviewed April 2022)
- https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/
- Key facts used: Colic usually stops by 3-4 months; hold upright during feeds, wind after feeds, gentle rocking, warm bath, white noise; see a GP if symptoms continue after 4 months; urgent help for a weak or high-pitched cry or a cry that does not sound like their usual cry.
